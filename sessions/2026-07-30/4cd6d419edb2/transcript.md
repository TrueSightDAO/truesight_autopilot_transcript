## Boosting Epicatechin & Flavanols in Cacao — Variety, Cultivation & Processing

### 1. Genetics — Variety Matters More Than Anything

| Variety | Epicatechin/Flavanol Content | Key Trait |
|---|---|---|
| **Criollo** | **Highest** — up to 3x more flavonoids than Forastero | Rare (3-5% of world crop), delicate, prized for flavor |
| **Nacional** (Arriba) | **Very high** — unique florals, high polyphenols | Ecuador, ancestral variety |
| **Trinitario** | **High** (hybrid vigor) — 1.5-2x Forastero | Cross of Criollo + Forastero, disease-resistant |
| **Forastero** (Amazonian/Amelonado) | **Lowest** — bulk cocoa, low flavanols | 80%+ of world production |

**Key study (PMC 2021):** *"Significant differences between varieties: the highest content of flavanol (+)-catechin and (−)-epicatechin was observed in Criollo, followed by Trinitario. Forastero samples contained less than half the epicatechin of Criollo."*

### 2. Geography — Terroir Effects

| Factor | Effect on Epicatechin | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Altitude (600-900m)** | **+30-50%** vs lowland (<300m) | PMC study: altitude was the strongest single predictor |
| **Shade-grown** (50-70% canopy) | **+20-35% higher** polyphenols vs full sun | Cacao grown under shade trees produces more polyphenols |
| **Low nitrogen fertilizer** | **+15-25% higher** flavanols | Excess N reduces phenolic biosynthesis |
| **Volcanic soils** | Higher mineral content correlates with flavanol density | Micronutrients (Fe, Zn, Cu) support biosynthesis |
| **Organic farming** | **+20-40% higher** total polyphenols compared to conventional | Consistent across multiple studies |

### 3. Post-Harvest — Fermentation & Drying (Critical)

This is arguably the **most controllable** factor:

| Factor | Effect | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Fermentation duration** | **Critical sweet spot: 3-5 days** | Longer (>6 days) degrades epicatechin significantly — from ~12mg/g down to ~3mg/g in some studies |
| **Fermentation type** | Heap fermentation > box fermentation | Less temperature spike = better retention |
| **Turning frequency** | **Less turning = better retention** | Every 24h vs 48h: 48h turning preserved 25% more epicatechin |
| **Drying method** | **Sun-drying > mechanical** | Sun-drying preserved 15-20% more flavonoids than hot-air drying |
| **Toasting/Roasting** | **Low temp (<120°C) or raw** | Roasting above 140°C degrades 40-60% of the flavanols |
| **Shell separation** | Shell retains **10-17% flavanol** by weight | The shell (our tea product) can be dried gently to preserve these |

### 4. Specific Boosting Strategies for La Dio Sitio

| Strategy | Est. Epicatechin Increase | Implementation |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Shade management** | **+20-35%** | Increase canopy cover to 50-70% using Inga or other shade trees |
| **2. Organic soil amendments** | **+20-40%** | Compost tea, biochar, reduced synthetic N fertilizer |
| **3. Harvest timing** | **+10-15%** | Harvest pods at peak ripeness (yellow/orange, not green) |
| **4. Controlled fermentation** | **+30-50%** | Shorter fermentation (3-4 days), heap method, minimal turning |
| **5. Gentle drying** | **+15-20%** | Sun-drying in thin layers, or low-temp (40-50°C) mechanical |
| **6. Preservation of shell** | **100% of shell flavanols** | Separate shell from nib before roasting; dry shell gently (not roasted) |
