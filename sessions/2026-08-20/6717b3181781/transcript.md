## State of the field
- **Proven**: recycled-plastic boatbuilding at small/commercial scale (Flipflopi taxi dhows, Plastic Whale fleet, HDPE workboats/barges). Material is genuinely durable — HDPE hulls outlast wood and fiberglass, are fully recyclable, and don't corrode.
- **Not yet done**: large ocean-going cargo barges from actual ocean-gyre plastic at industrial scale. The economics are the blocker — ocean plastic is degraded (UV/salt), requires heavy sorting/cleaning, and virgin HDPE is cheap. That's why most ocean-plastic builds are statement/awareness vessels or community-scale loops like Eco Brixs.

## Ethiopia — the technology landscape

**The likely thing you saw: the Gambella "Eco Boat" (Norwegian Church Aid, 2020)**
- NCA Ethiopia's Gambella Field Office built an **"Eco Boat" entirely from discarded plastic bottles**, first trial-tested around **Jewi and Nguenyyiel refugee camps** (Gambella region, western Ethiopia near the South Sudan border).
- Part of NCA's **"Waste for Value"** program — which recycled **410,955 kg of discarded PET bottles, jerrycans, metal scraps, and paper**, funded by BEWI Group.
- Same program built **eco-brick latrines from plastic bottles in Jewi Refugee Camp**. So the pattern is: bottle waste → eco-bricks / eco-boats / latrines, done at refugee-community scale.
- Related: an Ethiopian boat maker in **Bahir Dar** built a **floating house to attract tourists on Lake Tana** (covered by DW) — same floating-structure-from-waste family.

**The serious industrial tech: Kubik (Addis Ababa) — the one to watch**
- Founded 2021 by **Kidus Asfaw**; raised **~$5.2M** (Seed + $1.9M extension, 2023–2024).
- Converts **hard-to-recycle plastic waste** (polyethylene, polypropylene, polystyrene) into **interlocking bricks, columns, beams, and jambs** via a proprietary **extrusion-based upcycling process** (sort → clean → inspect → melt/reform) at a factory in **Adama Industrial Park**.
- Building without cement, aggregates, or steel — **~40% cheaper than masonry**, **5× lower carbon** than cement blocks, flame-resistant, durable.
- Currently recycles **~5,000 kg/day** (up to **45,000 kg/day** at capacity); partners with Addis Ababa municipality + corporates for waste supply; expanding Pan-African from 2025.
- This is the closest thing to an actual *technology platform* for turning plastic waste into structural material at industrial scale in Ethiopia.

**EON (Addis Ababa) — the traceability play**
- Early-stage startup turning Addis plastic waste into **recycled fiber and yarn** for Ethiopia's upholstery/furniture/textile manufacturers (import substitution).
- Differentiator: **blockchain-backed traceability / chain of custody** — a cryptographic guarantee tracking material from Addis streets to the factory floor, so Ethiopian manufacturers can export to EU/US markets without greenwashing accusations. 78% customer retention; JASIRI and ICE Addis backed it.
- Only ~9% of Ethiopia's plastic waste is currently recycled — big headroom.

**Eco-brick / bottle-house movement** (community tier)
- Ethiopian innovators building **houses from plastic bottles** (each bottle filled with mud/sand → "eco-brick"; a small house can use up to ~9,000 bottles); refugee-focused variants (Pendeza Shalters, MIT Solve solution).

## How this connects to your original question
The Ethiopia tech stack is essentially: **waste plastic → (1) extruded structural components (Kubik), (2) eco-bricks/bottles (community), (3) fiber with provenance (EON), (4) floating boats/houses (NCA Gambella, Bahir Dar)**. The *floating/barge* piece exists at pilot scale (Eco Boat, floating house); the *industrial-scale floating structure* from it hasn't been done yet — Kubik-grade extrusion is the enabling tech that could plausibly make an actual plastic-waste barge at scale (their interlocking components are already structural and buoyant-capable).
