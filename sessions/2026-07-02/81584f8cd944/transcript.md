## Is It Long Lasting? My Take

**The structural case is strong, but there are real risks.**

### Why it could last (the bull case)

| Factor | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Supply locked up** | 93% of combined DRAM production already shifted to HBM. New fab capacity takes 2-3 years to come online. |
| **Demand not peaking** | HBM demand growing 70% YoY in 2026. Customers (Nvidia, hyperscalers) are **reserving supply years ahead**. |
| **HBM4 transition** | The shift from HBM3E → HBM4 in 2027 requires entirely new packaging tech — another capacity crunch. |
| **Agentic AI** | The next wave (AI agents doing tasks autonomously) needs even more memory per inference. |
| **Analyst consensus** | BofA, Goldman, McKinsey all calling this a multi-year supercycle, not a 12-18 month upcycle. Supply constrained through **at least 2027**. |

### Why it could end badly (the risks)

| Risk | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Demand pull-forward** | Hyperscalers (MSFT, GOOG, AMZN, META) are spending like there's no tomorrow. If their AI ROI disappoints, they cut capex — and memory is the first thing to get cancelled. |
| **Commodity cycle reassertion** | Memory has always been boom-bust. When new fabs come online (2027-2028), oversupply could crash prices. The industry has never sustained these margins before. |
| **Geopolitical** | Samsung and SK Hynix are Korean. Any Taiwan strait escalation or US-China semiconductor war escalation disrupts the entire chain. |
| **Tech substitution** | If Nvidia or someone figures out a way to need less HBM per GPU (unlikely but possible), the demand math changes. |
| **Valuation insanity** | MU at 22x sales. SNDK similarly parabolic. If growth merely *decelerates* (not reverses), these stocks could halve on multiple compression alone. |

### Bottom Line

I think the **supercycle thesis is real through 2027** — the supply/demand math is structural, not speculative. The three memory makers have genuine pricing power for the first time in decades because AI training literally cannot happen without HBM.

**But the stocks have already priced a lot of it in.** MU is up 260% YTD. The risk isn't that the cycle ends — it's that growth decelerates from "absurd" to merely "amazing" and the market punishes the multiple.

If you want exposure, **SK Hynix** arguably has the best risk/reward — they're the technological leader in HBM and less hyped in US markets. But all three are real businesses with real demand, not meme stocks.
