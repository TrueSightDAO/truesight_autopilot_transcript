## Cacao Beans & the Brazil Section 301 Tariff — Verdict: **EXEMPT**

**Cocoa beans (HTS 1801.00.00) are explicitly exempt** from the 25% tariff. Here's the evidence chain:

### What the USTR Annex says

The Federal Register notice's Annex lists every exempt HTS subheading. **Chapter 18 (Cocoa and cocoa preparations) is fully included** in the exemption list:

| HTS Code | Description | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **1801.00.00** | Cocoa beans, whole or broken, raw or roasted | ✅ **Exempt** |
| 1802.00.00 | Cocoa shells, husks, skins and waste | ✅ Exempt |
| 1803.10.00 | Cocoa paste, not defatted | ✅ Exempt |
| 1803.20.00 | Cocoa paste, defatted | ✅ Exempt |
| 1804.00.00 | Cocoa butter, fat and oil | ✅ Exempt |
| 1805.00.00 | Cocoa powder, no sugar | ✅ Exempt |

This was confirmed in the **proposed** Annex (June 1), and the **final** Annex (July 15) retained all Chapter 18 lines — the only removals were high-purity dissolving pulp and non-pharma applications of certain chemicals. Cocoa was untouched.

### Why it's exempt

The USTR's four exemption criteria:
1. Raw materials critical to US supply chains
2. Products that could cause economy-wide disruptions
3. Items not available domestically or from alternative sources in sufficient quantities
4. Articles where tariffs wouldn't materially change Brazilian conduct

Cocoa beans fit #3 — the US doesn't grow cacao at commercial scale. There's no domestic alternative.

### What this means for TrueSight DAO

**No tariff impact on our cacao imports.** Our ceremonial cacao (raw beans → processed product) enters the US duty-free on the base MFN rate (0% for raw beans) AND is exempt from the 25% Section 301 surcharge.

**However, there are two things to watch:**

1. **The Section 122 surcharge (10%)** — this is a separate, temporary measure that expires **July 25**. For 4 days (July 22–25), both the 10% Section 122 AND the 25% Section 301 stack. But since cacao is exempt from the 301, it should only face the 122 during those 4 days — and that expires in 9 days anyway.

2. **The forced labor Section 301 probe** — a separate investigation concluding July 24 could add **another 12.5%** on top. Cacao supply chains are in scope for that one (West African cocoa is explicitly named in the probe docs). Brazil is also under investigation, but the outcome is unclear. This is the bigger medium-term risk.

### Bottom line

**Cacao is safe from this specific tariff.** The deforestation angle the US cited actually works in our favor — our traceable, regenerative supply chain is exactly the kind of provenance that could qualify for preferential treatment under future trade actions.
