6 · Variety A — CCN-51 “Ponta Verde”: history & agronomy
Origins (Ecuador, 1960s–1980s): CCN-51 (Colección Castro Naranjal 51) was bred by Ecuadorian agronomist
Homero Castro over ~12 years of crossing and selection. The “51” marks the 51st cross in the series; the clone derives
from a 3-way cross involving IMC 67 / ICS 95 / O-1 in its pedigree. First production reports date to 1965 (Finca Sofia,
Naranjal); the variety was released publicly in 1984. It was bred as a crisis response to Ecuador's witches' broom
(Moniliophthora perniciosa) and Ceratocystis wilt (mal del machete), and for high, reliable yield. Castro died in the late
1980s before patenting it, so the clone spread freely.

Genetics: genetic studies put CCN-51 at roughly 1.1% Nacional, 45.4% IMC, 22.2% Criollo, 21.5% Amelonado, 3.9%
Contamana, 2.5% Purús and 2.1% Marañon — essentially a forastero-dominant hybrid with a thin Nacional fraction.
Not fine-flavour or heirloom; the industry classifies it as “bulk cacao”. ~70% of Ecuadorian production and ~90% of new
plantings there over the last two decades; now spreading across Latin America and Africa.
Agronomy: yields ~700–1,100 kg/ha on farms (vs 300–400 kg/ha for Nacional) and up to 2–3 t/ha under ideal
conditions; Brazilian field trials recorded 2,143 kg/ha (year 6) and 2,955 kg/ha (year 7). Self-compatible, grows in full
sun, tolerates a wider disease load, starts producing ~2 years after planting — the traits behind year-round fruiting.
Registered among clonal cultivars (RNC/MAPA) in Brazil; common grafting choice in the Amazon (Pará) and Bahia.
“Ponta Verde”: the name used by the farmers — not an official registry denomination. Most plausibly a local/biotype
